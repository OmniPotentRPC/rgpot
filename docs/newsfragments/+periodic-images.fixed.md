Periodic kernels count a neighbour at every periodic image inside the cutoff, not only the nearest one, so a cell thinner than twice the cutoff (a one-atom fcc cell, a triclinic primitive cell) gives the energy, forces and stress of an explicit supercell. This covers LJ, Morse, Sutton-Chen, Stillinger-Weber, the Tersoff SiC kernel, the cell-list EAM, the Fortran gathers (EAM-Al, FeHe, CuH2, SW, EDIP, Lenosky) and the TIP4P, SPC/E and TIP4P-Pt water models, through every entry point: the C ABI, the RPC server, eOn and the ASE calculators. EDIP no longer rejects a thin cell. Cells at least twice the cutoff thick keep the minimum-image path; a sheared cell needs twice the cutoff plus skin. A sheared cell is binned by its face spacings rather than its row lengths, so supercells of a lower-triangular primitive cell (the 60 degree diamond cell as ASE's `standard_form` or LAMMPS writes it) no longer lose pairs: from 4x4x4 with the Tersoff SiC kernel, 5x5x5 with Stillinger-Weber, and 7x7x7 with the Fortran SW and Tersoff. `rgpot_nlist_build` finds every pair on sheared and thin cells at any atom count, including the 512-atom and larger path, and atoms outside the cell keep their partners on wide cells too. Sutton-Chen accepts a cell with zeros on its diagonal, such as an fcc primitive cell. `RgpotCalculator` no longer tiles thin cells itself, and `RgpotRpcCalculator` no longer takes a `cutoff`.
