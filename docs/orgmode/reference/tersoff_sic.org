* Tersoff silicon-carbon (1989)

=TersoffSiC= is the 1989 silicon, carbon, and silicon-carbide kernel
of J. Tersoff, Phys. Rev. B 39, 5566 (1989),
doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.39.5566.
The erratum, Phys. Rev. B 41, 3248 (1990),
doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.41.3248.2, sets carbon B to 346.74 eV and d to 4.3484.
Carbon beta is 1.5724e-7 and is dimensionless. It is the Table I value
in the NIST and LAMMPS transcriptions. The erratum does not print it.

=PotType::TersoffSi= and the engine name =tersoff= are one potential.
There is no =PotType::TersoffSiC=.
An all-silicon configuration, and a null species pointer, use the
Fortran gather in =TersoffPot=.
Any atomic number other than 14 uses this kernel.
Calling =TersoffSiC= directly always evaluates the 1989 form, including
an all-silicon cell, whose silicon column matches that gather.

Atomic number 14 is silicon and 6 is carbon.
Any other species is rejected.
lambda3 is zero for both elements, so the factor
exp(lambda3^m (rij - rik)^m) is identically one and is not evaluated.
The cross term uses chi = 0.9776.
lambda and the repulsive amplitude are arithmetic and geometric means.
The attractive amplitude is chi times the geometric mean.
The inner cutoffs are averaged with each other, and the outer cutoffs
are averaged with each other.

** Calls

Lengths are Angstrom and the energy is eV.
The cell is periodic in each direction.
=forceImpl= writes the force, which is minus the gradient.
=energyAndGradient= writes the energy and dE/dx for one configuration.
=energyAndGradientBatch= writes both for several configurations that
share one cell and one species list, so a caller enters the library
once per batch.
The Python method =energy_gradient= accepts positions of shape
=(n_atoms, 3)= or =(n_config, n_atoms, 3)= and uses that entry.

An orthogonal cell that holds at least three cutoff-lengths on each
edge builds the bond list once per call.
The cell edge is the silicon outer cutoff, the longest in this set, or
that cutoff plus the skin of a =nlist::NeighborList=.
A null list rebuilds on every call.
A held list is reused while each atom has moved by at most half the skin.
Any other cell uses the minimum-image fold.

** Held list on the force path

=forceImpl= rebuilds the pairs on every call by default.
=setHeldSkin(skin)= with a positive skin in Angstrom makes the instance
keep one =nlist::NeighborList= across =forceImpl= calls.
The list is rebuilt when any atom has moved by more than half the skin
since the last build, or when the cell or the atom count changes.
=setHeldSkin(0)= drops the list and goes back to a rebuild per call.
A negative or non-finite skin throws =std::invalid_argument=.
=heldList()= returns the list, so a caller can see when it was last built.

With a positive skin the instance reports =Reentrancy::PerInstance= and
=perImageInstances=, since a call writes the list.
=TersoffPot::setHeldSkin= passes the skin to its own 1989 kernel.
All-silicon calls through =TersoffPot= stay on the Fortran gather, which
keeps its own vesin table with a skin of 0.3 Angstrom.

On the call that builds the list, and on a call with the coordinates of
that build, a held list returns the same bits as a rebuild.
After a move inside the skin the bits also match while every atom stays
in its cell of the cutoff-edged grid that orders the pairs.
An atom that crosses a cell face keeps the pair order of the last build,
so the bonds of a centre can be summed in another order.
=TersoffSiCTest= bounds that difference by 1e-12 on 512 and 4096 atoms.
=tersoff_held_bench= reports the largest difference it sees and the time
per atom of a rebuild, a held list, and the Fortran gather.

** Stress

=TersoffSiC::forceImpl= writes the Cauchy stress, sets =has_stress=, and
=caps().stress= is true, with or without a held list.
The convention is the one of =LJPot=, =MorsePot= and =ZBLPot=, set in
=stress.hpp=.
The stress is sigma = dE/dε / V for the strain that sends every position and
every cell row r to r (I + ε).
It is row-major and symmetric, in eV/Angstrom^3.
A compressed cell has negative diagonal entries, the sign ASE uses.
For the LAMMPS virial W, sigma = -W / V.

The virial adds d ⊗ g for each folded bond vector d, where g is the energy
gradient with respect to d.
Each bond i-j adds its radial term, and each bond i-k in the bond order of i-j
adds the angular and cutoff terms of both bonds.
That sum is the many-body virial of Thompson, Plimpton and Mattson, J. Chem.
Phys. 131, 154107 (2009), doi:10.1063/1.3245303.
=energyAndGradient= and the batch call skip the virial, and the energy and the
forces keep their bits.

=TersoffSiCTest= checks all nine entries against a fourth-order central
difference of the energy in the strain, step 2e-4.
The cells are compressed cubic silicon, a sheared silicon cell, and a sheared,
rattled silicon carbide cell whose Si-Si pairs sit inside the cutoff taper.
The tolerance is 5e-10 eV/Angstrom^3.

=TersoffPot= keeps =caps().stress= false, since its all-silicon calls run the
Fortran gather, which writes no stress.
A call with any other species reaches this kernel, and =TersoffPot= then drops
its stress.
Every =TersoffPot= call leaves =has_stress= at 0 and the nine entries at zero,
so a reused output keeps no tensor from an earlier call.

** Diamond energies

At a0 = 5.432 Angstrom the silicon diamond energy is -4.629595 eV/atom.
At a0 = 3.5656 Angstrom the carbon diamond energy is -7.370514 eV/atom.
Those are the closed forms of this parameter set at the lattice
constants that reproduce the fitted cohesive energies, 4.63 eV and
7.37 eV.
=TersoffSiCTest= pins both and checks the analytic gradient against a
finite difference.
