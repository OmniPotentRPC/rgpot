* Tersoff silicon-carbon (1989)

=TersoffSiC= is the 1989 silicon, carbon, and silicon-carbide kernel of J.
Tersoff, Phys.
Rev. B 39, 5566 (1989),
doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.39.5566. The erratum, Phys. Rev. B 41, 3248 (1990), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.41.3248.2,
sets carbon B to 346.74 eV and d to 4.3484.
Carbon beta is 1.5724e-7 and is dimensionless.
It is the Table I value in the NIST and LAMMPS transcriptions.
The erratum does not print it.

=PotType::TersoffSi= and the engine name =tersoff= are one potential.
There is no =PotType::TersoffSiC=.
An all-silicon configuration, and a null species pointer, use the Fortran gather
in =TersoffPot=.
Any atomic number other than 14 uses this kernel.
Calling =TersoffSiC= directly always evaluates the 1989 form, including an
all-silicon cell, whose silicon column matches that gather.

Atomic number 14 is silicon and 6 is carbon.
Any other species is rejected. lambda3 is zero for both elements, so the factor
exp(lambda3^m (rij - rik)^m) is identically one and is not evaluated.
The cross term uses chi = 0.9776. lambda and the repulsive amplitude are
arithmetic and geometric means.
The attractive amplitude is chi times the geometric mean.
The inner cutoffs are averaged with each other, and the outer cutoffs are
averaged with each other.

** Calls

Lengths are Angstrom and the energy is eV.
The cell is periodic in each direction.
=forceImpl= writes the force, which is minus the gradient.
=energyAndGradient= writes the energy and dE/dx for one configuration.
=energyAndGradientBatch= writes both for several configurations that share one
cell and one species list, so a caller enters the library once per batch.
The Python method =energy_gradient= accepts positions of shape =(n_atoms, 3)= or
=(n_config, n_atoms, 3)= and uses that entry.

An orthogonal cell that holds at least three cutoff-lengths on each edge builds
the bond list once per call.
The cell edge is the silicon outer cutoff, the longest in this set, or that
cutoff plus the skin of a =nlist::NeighborList=.
A null list rebuilds on every call.
A held list is reused while each atom has moved by at most half the skin.
Any other cell uses the minimum-image fold.

** Held list on the force path

=forceImpl= rebuilds the pairs on every call by default.
=setHeldSkin(skin)= with a positive skin in Angstrom makes the instance keep one
=nlist::NeighborList= across =forceImpl= calls.
The list is rebuilt when any atom has moved by more than half the skin since the
last build, or when the cell or the atom count changes.
=setHeldSkin(0)= drops the list and goes back to a rebuild per call.
A negative or non-finite skin throws =std::invalid_argument=.
=heldList()= returns the list, so a caller can see when it was last built.

With a positive skin the instance reports =Reentrancy::PerInstance= and
=perImageInstances=, since a call writes the list.
=TersoffPot::setHeldSkin= passes the skin to its own 1989 kernel.
All-silicon calls through =TersoffPot= stay on the Fortran gather, which keeps
its own vesin table with a skin of 0.3 Angstrom.

On the call that builds the list, and on a call with the coordinates of that
build, a held list returns the same bits as a rebuild.
After a move inside the skin the bits also match while every atom stays in its
cell of the cutoff-edged grid that orders the pairs.
An atom that crosses a cell face keeps the pair order of the last build, so the
bonds of a centre can be summed in another order.
=TersoffSiCTest= bounds that difference by 1e-12 on 512 and 4096 atoms.
=tersoff_held_bench= reports the largest difference it sees and the time per
atom of a rebuild, a held list, and the Fortran gather.

** Diamond energies

At a0 = 5.432 Angstrom the silicon diamond energy is -4.629595
eV/atom. At a0 = 3.5656 Angstrom the carbon diamond energy is -7.370514 eV/atom.
Those are the closed forms of this parameter set at the lattice constants that
reproduce the fitted cohesive energies, 4.63 eV and 7.37 eV.
=TersoffSiCTest= pins both and checks the analytic gradient against a finite
difference.
