* Stillinger-Weber silicon (1985)

=StillingerWeberSi= evaluates the silicon potential of F. H. Stillinger
and T. A. Weber, Phys. Rev. B 31, 5262 (1985),
doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.31.5262.

The two-body and three-body parameters are equation (2.7).
Sigma is 0.20951 nm.
Epsilon is the value equation (2.9) prints, 3.4723e-12 erg per atom
pair, converted with 1 erg = 1e-7 J and the CODATA electron-volt
(1.602176634e-19 J).
The Fortran =SWPot= class keeps eOn's rescaled epsilon and is not this
potential.

** Calls

Lengths are Angstrom and the energy is eV.
The cell is periodic in each direction.
=forceImpl= writes the force, which is minus the gradient.
=energyAndGradient= writes the energy and dE/dx for one configuration.
=energyAndGradientBatch= writes both for several configurations that
share one cell and one species list, so a caller enters the library
once per batch.
The Python method =energy_gradient= accepts positions of shape
=(n_atoms, 3)= or =(n_config, n_atoms, 3)= and uses that entry.

An orthogonal cell that holds at least three cutoff-lengths on each
edge builds one half-shell pair list per call.
The cell edge is the cutoff, or the cutoff plus the skin of a
=nlist::NeighborList= passed to =energyAndGradient=.
The list is reused while every atom has moved by at most half that
skin.
A null list rebuilds every call.
Any other cell uses the minimum-image fold.

** Diamond energy

At the tetrahedral angle the three-body term is zero, and the diamond
minimum puts every nearest neighbour at the minimum of the pair term,
where that term equals -1.
Each atom then has two bonds, so the energy per atom is -2 epsilon.
Equation (2.8) prints -1.999993 epsilon for the lattice sum at the
density quoted there.
=SWSiTest= checks both figures, and checks the analytic gradient
against a finite difference.
