* Quantum Sutton-Chen potential

=QSCPot= (=PotType::QSC=) evaluates the multi-element Quantum Sutton-Chen (Q-SC)
many-body potential of Qi, Cagin, Kimura and Goddard, Physical Review B 59, 3527
(1999).

doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.59.3527

Eqs. (1a) to (1c) use the combination rules of Eq. (2).
Rafii-Tabar and Sutton give those rules in Philosophical Magazine Letters 63,
217 (1991).

doi:10.1080/09500839108205994

#+attr_rgpot: skip the energy formula, not code
#+begin_example
E = sum_{i < j} epsilon_ab * (a_ab / r_ij)^(n_ab)
    - sum_i c_a * epsilon_aa * sqrt(rho_i)
rho_i = sum_{j != i} (a_ab / r_ij)^(m_ab)
#+end_example

where =a= is the species of atom =i= and =b= is the species of atom =j=.
For unlike species =a != b= the pair parameters follow Eq. (2) of the paper:

#+attr_rgpot: skip the mixing rules, not code
#+begin_example
epsilon_ab = sqrt(epsilon_a * epsilon_b)
a_ab = (a_a + a_b) / 2
n_ab = (n_a + n_b) / 2
m_ab = (m_a + m_b) / 2
#+end_example

The density term uses the mixed =a_ab= and =m_ab= exactly like the repulsive
term, so each pair adds the same amount to =rho_i= and =rho_j=.
There is no combination rule for =c=: it belongs to the atom whose density is
evaluated.
The quantum correction of the Q-SC force field sits in the fitted parameters
(they include the zero-point energy), so the functional form is that of
Sutton-Chen and the energy has no extra term.
The repulsive and density exponents =n_ab= and =m_ab= can be half-integers on an
alloy pair such as copper and silver, where =n_Cu = 10= and =n_Ag = 11= give
=n_CuAg = 10.5=.
The paper truncates each pair at =2 a_ab=; =QSCConfig::cutoff= is one global
distance instead.

** Parameter table and reference values

The default table carries the eight fcc transition-metal rows fitted with
zero-point quantum corrections.
The Ni and Cu rows are Table I of Qi, Cagin, Kimura and Goddard, Physical Review
B 59, 3527 (1999),
doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.59.3527. The other rows follow the eight-metal table of Ozdemir Kart, Tomak, Uludogan and Cagin, Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids 337, 101 (2004), doi:10.1016/j.jnoncrysol.2004.03.121,
Table 1, as tabulated in eOn =client/potentials/QSC/Parameters.h=.
Two rows differ from eOn's table or from Table I and are recorded here so that
nobody has to rediscover them. eOn lists =a= = 3.5200 Angstrom for Cu; Table I
gives 3.6030, which is used.

below keeps the eight-metal values for Ag.

A homonuclear dimer at separation =r = a= has energy =epsilon * (1 - 2 c)= and a
force =epsilon * (m * c - n) / a= on the atom at the origin toward its partner.
The energies are -1.2429001830 eV for nickel and -0.9770461806 eV for copper;
the forces are 0.8680846510 and 0.6658825150
eV/Angstrom. =QSCPotTest= checks all eight homonuclear dimers to 1e-14 eV and all eight fcc crystals at the paper's =2 a= cutoff (8 neighbour shells, 140 neighbours) to 1e-11 eV/atom.
=setElementParameter= overrides an existing element or adds a new atomic number.
With the reduced 10-8 parameters of Sutton and Chen, Philosophical Magazine
Letters 61, 139 (1990), doi:10.1080/09500839008206493, =QSCPot= reproduces the
deposited Doye and Wales =Pt38= (-10117.2454) and =Pt75= (-20431.3452) minima
and matches =SuttonChenPot= bit for bit.

** Reference data and cross-checks

Three kinds of data back the tests, all generated by scripts under
=scripts/qsc_reference/= and stored in =CppCore/tests/data/qsc/=.
Each data file starts with a header that records how it was made.

- =eon_3b2586f_pure.dat= holds energies and forces (and no stress) of the real
  eOn =QSC= class at eOn commit 3b2586f543b82cbf8dcdcbd07f2e1126705a3826 for
  pure elements only: dimers and 15-atom clusters of all eight metals, and
  108-atom periodic cells of Ni, Cu, Ag and Au.
  =gen_eon_pure.py= builds eOn's own =client/potentials/QSC/QSC.cpp= with a
  small driver (=eon_qsc_driver.cpp=), checks that =-O0= and =-O2= builds agree
  bit for bit, and records the compiler and the full compile line.
  The Cu =a= value is overridden with =set_qsc_parameter= to the Table I value.
  =QSCPotTest= and =tests/python/test_qsc_reference.py= compare =QSCPot= with it
  to 1e-12 relative, and the file agrees with the published equations to about
  1e-15.
- =oracle_mixtures.dat= holds energies, forces and the six stress components of mixtures from =qsc_oracle.py=.
  The oracle evaluates Eqs. (1a) to (1c) and (2) of the paper in 50-digit
  =decimal= arithmetic, sums every periodic image inside the cutoff, and checks
  its analytic forces and stress against central differences in the same
  arithmetic (energy for forces, strain for stress).
  It has six unlike dimers, a 19-atom eight-metal cluster, and an orthorhombic
  and a triclinic periodic cell of 108 atoms.
- =lammps_crosscheck.py= compares the same mixture data with LAMMPS =pair_style eam/fs= tables, which can hold this potential because the density may depend on the element pair (=eam/alloy=
  cannot).
  Table interpolation limits the agreement: energies match to 3e-13 relative,
  forces to 2e-8 and stresses to 1.3e-9.
  It needs LAMMPS and is not part of the test suite.

The derivative checks in =QSCPotTest= (central differences of the energy against
the forces, of the energy against strain for the stress, and the per-atom virial
sum) run on the mixtures as well.

** Mixtures differ from eOn

eOn 3b2586f does not evaluate the equations above for mixed-species systems, and
the mixture tests make no comparison with it.
Pure elements agree with eOn to rounding; mixtures do not, for two separate
reasons.

1. Parameter caching.
   In =QSC::energy= (=QSC.cpp= lines 137 to 157) and =QSC::force= (lines 203 to
   223),
   =p_ii= is refreshed only when the species of atom =i= differs from the previous atom, and =p_ij=
   and =p_jj= are refreshed only when the species of neighbour =j= differs from
   the previous neighbour, whatever the atom =i= is.
   A pair parameter depends on both species, so =p_ij= goes stale whenever =i=
   changes species while the last neighbour keeps its own; the three variables
   are also uninitialised locals, so their value on a skipped refresh is
   whatever the stack held.
   A pure element never changes species and is not affected.
2. Density term. eOn adds =(a_j / r)^m_j= (the neighbour's own parameters,
   =p_jj= and =p_ii= at lines 160 to 163) to the density, where Eq. (1c) uses
   =(a_ij / r)^m_ij=.
   This changes every unlike pair, with or without the caching problem.

Both effects were measured with =scripts/qsc_reference/eon_mixture_probe.py=,
which runs eOn 3b2586f, a copy of it without the caching, and the oracle with
either density rule on the orthorhombic mixtures.
Against the equations above the largest energy deviation of eOn is 0.19 eV on a
Cu-Ag dimer, 5.0 eV on the 19-atom cluster and 90 eV on the 108-atom cell.
Removing the caching changes the cluster by 0.007 eV and the cell by 4.4 eV;
with the caching removed and the neighbour density rule, the copy of eOn matches
the oracle's neighbour-rule evaluation to 2e-13 eV in energy and 1e-14
eV/Angstrom in force.
=QSCPot= therefore follows the published equations, and its mixtures are not
expected to equal eOn's.

** Neighbour list, stress, staging and Hessian-vector products

Pairs come from =rgpot::nlist::Neighbours= with a Verlet skin of
=QSCConfig::verlet_skin= (0.5 Angstrom by default, 0 in =nlist::Mode::Stream=).
A cutoff of 0 or less with =periodic= false keeps every pair.
With =QSCConfig::stress= set, =forceImpl= writes the Cauchy stress of Thompson,
Plimpton and Mattson, Journal of Chemical Physics 131, 154107 (2009),
doi:10.1063/1.3245303.
=forcePerAtom= splits each pair energy and virial half to each atom and puts the
embedding energy on its own atom.
=locality=, =densityStage= and =forceStage= evaluate a domain-decomposed host's
half list by exchanging one scalar density =rho_i= per atom.
=hessianVectorImpl= evaluates the exact analytic second-derivative product =H v=
in two passes over the pairs, including the embedding curvature term
=F''(rho_i) (d rho_i / dt)=, with zero force calls.

#+attr_rgpot: run link=rgpot
#+begin_src cpp
#include <cstdio>

#include "rgpot/QSC/QSCPot.hpp"

int main() {
  rgpot::QSCPot pot({.cutoff = 8.0, .periodic = false});
  const double pos[6] = {0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 2.65, 0.0, 0.0};
  const int z[2] = {29, 47};
  const double box[9] = {30.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 30.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 30.0};
  double forces[6] = {};
  rgpot::ForceInput in{2, pos, z, box};
  rgpot::ForceOut out{};
  out.F = forces;
  pot.forceImpl(in, &out);
  std::printf("Cu-Ag dimer energy: %.6f eV\n", out.energy);
  return 0;
}
#+end_src
